  1. 1

    Peer-reviewed and independently analyzed.

    Published in Nature Medicine. The study was led and analyzed independently by University College London.

  2. 2

    Vast majority of participants stayed on the treatment.

    91.4% were still on treatment at six months. Discontinuation was lower among those using structured side-effect support (5.0% vs 7.4%).

  3. 3

    Results were achieved below the maximum dose.

    Average dose exposure across the study sat at around 63% of the maximum licensed dose, and weight loss over six months was comparable to the trajectories reported in the major clinical trials of these treatments.

  4. 4

    Blood sugar and cholesterol improved alongside weight.

    84.5% of those in the diabetes range and 96.3% of those with prediabetes returned to normal blood sugar. Average total cholesterol moved into the healthy range.

  5. 5

    Outcomes were consistent across the population.

    No significant difference in weight loss by deprivation level, and results held at every age from 18 to 79. There were no serious treatment-related adverse events.
